### 3.2 The market
- **Indian streetwear:** estimated at **USD 23.1 bn (2025)**, projected to reach about USD 43 bn by 2034 (~7.1% CAGR, Deep Market Insights). This is a single-firm estimate.
- **Online fashion in India:** about **USD 11 bn**, projected at **USD 35 bn by 2028** (Entrepreneur India).
- **D2C:** fashion is one of the largest D2C categories in India. Growth is driven by Instagram campaigns, creator collabs and **social-led drops** instead of seasonal calendars (Fibre2Fashion).
- **Peer / competitor brands** (content benchmarks): Jaywalking, Almost Gods, Huemn, Capsul, Urban Monkey, Bonkers Corner, The Souled Store, Snitch (mass), plus global references (Fear of God Essentials, Corteiz, Represent, Aimé Leon Dore, Stüssy).

### 3.3 The platform ecosystem
| Channel | How it works today | Implication for content |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram organic** | Reels, carousels, single images, stories, Broadcast channels, Collabs posts. Reach is algorithmic and driven by watch time, shares and saves | Hook in the **first ~3–4 s** matters. Shares/saves > likes. Series ("IPs") build habit and recall |
| **Meta Ads** | Advantage+ automation. Targeting is increasingly done **by the creative itself** ("creative is the new targeting") | The team's main lever is **creative diversity**: different angles, hooks and formats, not just different product shots |
| **Creative fatigue** | Performance drops as frequency rises (often flagged above ~3 frequency, or after weeks 3–4 of prospecting) | A pipeline of fresh variations is needed, and the team needs to know *when* to refresh |

**Key benchmark signals (directional):**
- Fashion brands: **Reels ~1.9% engagement vs ~0.8%** for carousels/static, and higher reach per post (Dash Social fashion data via NetInfluencer).
- All industries / large accounts: **carousels often win** on engagement and reach (Socialinsider 2026; Dash Social H2 2025).
- Engagement is **declining year on year** across formats (Socialinsider).
- Meta reports **diversified creative can improve CPA by up to 32%** and incremental reach by 9% compared with similar-looking creative (via B&T / AdNews).
- UGC-style ads often beat polished brand ads on CTR/CVR for D2C (vendor data, figures vary by source).

➡️ **Inference:** No single format "wins". It depends on the brand, account size and goal. That is exactly why BLUORNG needs a tool that learns from **its own** data, not generic rules.

## 4. Literature Review

| # | Source | Key idea | Relevance to BLUPRINT |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kaplan & Haenlein (2010), *Users of the world, unite!*, Business Horizons | Social media as a set of platforms built on user-generated content; brands must take part, not broadcast | Organic content must be participatory (Story Experience, UGC) |
| 2 | Berger & Milkman (2012), *What makes online content viral?*, J. of Marketing Research | High-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, amusement) drive sharing more than low-arousal ones | Recommendation engine should tag content by **emotion/arousal**, not just format |
| 3 | de Vries, Gensler & Leeflang (2012), *Popularity of brand posts on brand fan pages*, J. of Interactive Marketing | Vividness and interactivity of posts affect likes and comments differently | Tag posts by vividness (video/static) and interactivity (polls/questions) |
| 4 | Ashley & Tuten (2015), *Creative strategies in social media marketing*, Psychology & Marketing | Brands use different message strategies (functional, emotional, experiential, user-image) | Basis for the platform's **creative-angle taxonomy** |
| 5 | Kim & Ko (2012), *Do social media marketing activities enhance customer equity?* (luxury fashion), J. of Business Research | Entertainment, interaction, trendiness, customisation and word-of-mouth drive equity for luxury fashion | The five dimensions become content-goal tags for a premium brand |
| 6 | Godey et al. (2016), *Social media marketing efforts of luxury brands*, J. of Business Research | Social media efforts build brand awareness and image, which drive preference and loyalty | Supports measuring **recall/brand-building**, not only sales |
| 7 | Cialdini (1984/2021), *Influence* — Scarcity principle | Limited availability increases perceived value | Drop model: scarcity content must be timed and not overused (fatigue) |
| 8 | Lemon & Verhoef (2016), *Understanding customer experience throughout the customer journey*, J. of Marketing | Touchpoints across pre-purchase, purchase and post-purchase | Map content types to the **audience journey** (discover → desire → drop → wear → share) |
| 9 | Meta (2023–25), *Creative is the new targeting* / creative diversification guidance | Diverse creative improves CPA and reach. Similar openers are treated as near-duplicates | Paid module must push **hook and angle diversity** and flag sameness |
| 10 | Amershi et al. (2019), *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction*, CHI | 18 guidelines: make clear what the system can do, show why, support correction, learn from feedback | Core UX principles: **explainable recommendations**, editable, feedback loop |
| 11 | Davis (1989), *Technology Acceptance Model* | Adoption depends on perceived usefulness and ease of use | The team will only adopt it if it saves time **and** gives better ideas than WhatsApp + gut |
| 12 | Nielsen (1994), *10 Usability Heuristics* | Visibility, match with real world, control, consistency… | Evaluation framework for later usability testing |

### 4.1 Literature synthesis
1. **Content success is multi-dimensional:** format, emotion, interactivity, timing and creative angle all matter (2, 3, 4).
2. **Luxury/premium fashion has different goals:** brand equity and desire matter as much as clicks (5, 6), so the tool must not chase vanity metrics or cheapen the brand.
3. **Scarcity is powerful but fragile** (7). Drop content needs planning around a calendar.
4. **Paid performance now depends on creative variety** (9). Teams need help producing *different* ideas, not more of the same.
5. **AI recommendations must be explainable and controllable** (10, 11), otherwise creative teams reject them.
