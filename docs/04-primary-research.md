# Part 4 — Primary Research: Plan, User Study, Instruments, Report & Inferences

> **Read this first:** This part gives you the research **plan, instruments, analysis method and report structure**, filled with **hypotheses** where the results will go. You must run the research yourself; the jury will check that the data is real. Every `[ ]` / `▢` cell is a slot for your real findings. Hypotheses (H1–H10) are what we *expect* to find. Your data will confirm or reject them, and a rejected hypothesis is a good finding too.

## 9. Primary Research Plan

### 9.1 Research questions
| # | Research question | Method(s) |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | How does BLUORNG's team currently decide what content to make (organic & paid)? | Contextual inquiry, shadowing, interviews |
| RQ2 | Which data do they look at, how often, and what do they do with it? | Interviews, survey (team/industry) |
| RQ3 | Where are the biggest time, money and frustration losses in ideation → brief → shoot → post → measure? | Shadowing a drop week, journey mapping, survey |
| RQ4 | How do organic and paid teams coordinate (or not)? | Interviews with both roles |
| RQ5 | What content does BLUORNG's audience engage with, save, share, and buy from, and why? | Audience survey, focus group, content audit |
| RQ6 | What would make the team trust (or reject) AI/data-based content recommendations? | Interviews, concept test |

### 9.2 Hypotheses
| ID | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H1 | Most content decisions are made on intuition/trends, not on structured data |
| H2 | Teams check performance data, but fewer than half use it to plan the *next* content |
| H3 | Organic winners are rarely turned into ads systematically |
| H4 | Performance marketers feel they don't get enough ad creative variations |
| H5 | Vague briefs cause re-shoots/re-edits at least occasionally |
| H6 | Drop weeks create last-minute content pressure |
| H7 | The audience engages more with behind-the-scenes, craft/detail (ASMR) and styling content than with plain product posts |
| H8 | The audience dislikes ads that look like ads, and prefers creator/UGC-style ones |
| H9 | Teams would trust AI suggestions only if they're explained and editable |
| H10 | Brand consistency ("not looking mass") is a top concern for premium brands |

### 9.3 Methods & sample
| Method | Who | Sample | Duration | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Content audit** (secondary/primary) | BLUORNG IG: last 90 posts + 30 ads (with permission) + 3 peer brands | 90 + 30 posts | 1 week | Tagged spreadsheet: format, IP, hook, emotion, goal → metrics |
| **Survey A: Practitioners** | Social media managers, content creators, cinematographers, performance marketers at fashion/D2C brands (incl. BLUORNG team) | 20–40 | 10 min | Quant data on workflow & pain |
| **Survey B: Audience** | 18–30 streetwear followers (BLUORNG followers via story link + college + streetwear communities) | 100–150 | 5–7 min | What content people engage with and buy from |
| **In-depth interviews** | BLUORNG founder/brand head, social manager, perf. marketer, editor + 3–4 external practitioners | 6–8 | 30–45 min | Qualitative insights, quotes |
| **Focus group** | 6–8 audience members (BLUORNG buyers + followers) | 1–2 groups | 60 min | Reactions to content types & ads |
| **Ethnography / shadowing** | Shadow one full **drop week** (planning meeting → shoot → edit → posting → ad launch) | 1 drop cycle | 5–7 days | Observation log, photos, time-on-task, journey map |
| **Concept probe** (end of research) | 4–5 team members | 4–5 | 20 min | Reactions to 3 rough concept sketches |

### 9.4 Ethics
- Mentor approval for internal data. Consent at the start of every survey/interview. Anonymise names ("P1, P2…"). No personal audience data stored. Record only with permission.

## 11. Research Instruments

### 11.1 Survey A: Practitioners (Google Form)
*Intro:* "I'm a design student researching how fashion brands decide what content to create. 10 minutes, anonymous."

**Section 1: About you**

1. Your role? *(Social media manager / Content strategist / Cinematographer-photographer / Editor / Performance marketer / Founder / Agency / Other)*
2. Brand type? *(Streetwear / Premium fashion / Mass fashion / Other D2C)*
3. Team size? *(1–3 / 4–10 / 11–25 / 25+)*
4. Years of experience? *(<1 / 1–3 / 3–5 / 5+)*

**Section 2: How you decide content**

5. How do you mainly decide what content to make? *(multi-select: Gut/experience · Trends/audio · Competitors · Past performance data · Founder/manager decides · Agency suggests · Audience comments/DMs)*
6. How often do you look at content performance data? *(Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely / Never)*
7. When you look at data, do you use it to plan the next content? *(Always / Often / Sometimes / Rarely / Never)*
8. Which tools do you use? *(IG Insights · Meta Ads Manager · Meta Business Suite · Later/Hootsuite/Sprout · Notion · Google Sheets · WhatsApp · ChatGPT · Motion/Foreplay · Other)*
9. Rate (1–5): "I know clearly which *type* of content works best for our brand."
10. Rate (1–5): "Coming up with fresh content ideas every week is hard."

**Section 3: Organic ↔ paid**

11. How often are your best organic posts turned into ads? *(Always … Never)*
12. Rate (1–5): "Organic and paid teams plan content together."
13. *(Perf. marketers)* How many new ad creatives do you test per month? *(0–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / 10+)*
14. *(Perf. marketers)* Rate (1–5): "I get enough creative variations to test."
15. How do you notice an ad is fatigued? *(open)*

**Section 4: Brief & production**

16. How are content briefs usually shared? *(Verbal · WhatsApp · Doc/Notion · Deck · No brief)*
17. How often do vague briefs cause re-shoots or re-edits? *(Very often … Never)*
18. Rate (1–5): "Drop weeks are stressful for content."

**Section 5: Pain & wishes**

19. Rank your top 3 problems: *(Idea block · Not knowing what works · Too little time · Too few ad variations · Unclear briefs · Approval delays · Staying on-brand · Reporting takes time)*
20. Would you trust content suggestions from an AI tool? *(Yes / Only if it explains why / Only as inspiration / No)*
21. What must such a tool NEVER do? *(open)*
22. If you had a magic content assistant, what's the first thing it would do for you? *(open)*

### 11.2 Survey B: Audience
1. Age · 2. City · 3. Do you follow BLUORNG? · 4. Have you bought from BLUORNG? *(Yes / Planning to / No)*
5. Where do you **discover** new streetwear brands? *(IG Reels · IG Explore · Friends · Creators · Ads · Stores · Reddit/Discord · Other)*
6. Which content from fashion brands do you **watch fully**? *(multi: Drop teasers · Lookbooks · BTS/studio · ASMR/fabric close-ups · Styling guides · Fit checks/street style · Memes/culture · Collabs · Founder stories · Interactive stories)*
7. Which do you **save or share**? *(same list)*
8. Which content made you **buy** (or want to)? *(same list + open)*
9. Rate (1–5): "Brand ads on Instagram annoy me."
10. Which ads make you stop scrolling? *(Creator wearing it · Close-up details · Drop countdown · Store/queue hype · Model lookbook · Price/offer)*
11. How often should a streetwear brand post? *(Daily · 3–5/week · 1–2/week)*
12. Do you take part in polls/quizzes in brand stories? *(Often · Sometimes · Never)*
13. One thing you'd love BLUORNG to post more of? *(open)*
14. One thing you'd want them to stop? *(open)*

### 11.3 Interview guide (team / practitioners): 30–45 min
1. **Warm-up:** Walk me through your role and a normal week.
2. **Last piece of content:** Tell me about the last Reel/ad you worked on. Where did the idea come from? Who decided? What happened after?
3. **Data:** What numbers do you check? Show me (screen-share). What do you do with them?
4. **Wins & losses:** Tell me about a post/ad that did really well. Why do you think it did? Did you make more like it? And one that flopped?
5. **Organic ↔ paid:** How does content move from Instagram to ads (or the reverse)?
6. **Briefs:** Show me a recent brief. What's usually missing?
7. **Drops:** Walk me through the last drop week, day by day.
8. **Brand:** How do you know something is "BLUORNG" or "not BLUORNG"?
9. **AI/tools:** Have you used ChatGPT or any tool for ideas? What was good or bad?
10. **Magic wand:** If one part of this process could be fixed, which?

*Founder variant:* Add "What makes you approve or reject a content idea?" and "How do you judge if content is working: sales, buzz, or feel?"

### 11.4 Focus group guide (audience): 60 min
1. Ice-breaker: Your favourite streetwear brand on IG and why.
2. **Card sort:** Show 15 printed BLUORNG and peer-brand posts/ads → group them into "would watch / save / share / buy / skip".
3. Discuss: What makes a brand post feel premium vs cheap? Ad vs content?
4. Show 3 ad styles (studio lookbook vs creator UGC vs ASMR detail) and discuss reactions.
5. Co-create: "Design BLUORNG's next IG series."

### 12.4 Qualitative analysis method
1. Transcribe interviews → pull **quotes on sticky notes** (Miro/FigJam).
2. **Affinity map** into themes (use the clusters A–H in Part 3 as a starting frame and add new ones).
3. Count frequency (how many participants mention each theme) → **priority**.
4. Turn each theme into an **insight statement**: *"[User] needs [need] because [insight]."*

### 12.5 Expected insight statements (validate or rewrite with your data)
1. **"Data without direction."** The team sees numbers, but nobody turns them into "make more of X, less of Y".
2. **"The idea treadmill."** A drop-led brand needs constant novelty, and idea block peaks right before drops.
3. **"Two teams, one feed."** Organic and paid share the same audience but not the same plan.
4. **"Same shoot, same ads."** Ad variations are limited by what was shot, so planning ad angles *before* the shoot is the real fix.
5. **"Brief is a WhatsApp voice note."** Makers lose time to unclear briefs and re-edits.
6. **"Protect the cool."** Any suggestion that feels mass, salesy or trend-chasing will be rejected.
7. **"Show me why."** Trust in AI depends on explanation, examples from the brand's own past, and the ability to edit.

### 12.6 Priority areas of concern (shortlist)
| Rank | Area | Why it's priority | Design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Knowing what works (by *type* of content) | Root cause for everything else | Auto-tagging + "Why it worked" insights |
| 2 | Ideation for organic IPs | Highest-frequency pain | IP/series recommender + idea bank |
| 3 | Ad creative diversity & fatigue | Direct money impact | Angle × hook matrix, fatigue alerts |
| 4 | Organic → paid bridge | Quick win | "Promote this winner" + shared calendar |
| 5 | Briefs | Saves maker time | One-click brief with shot list |
| 6 | Brand guardrails | Adoption condition | Brand DNA + brand-fit score |

## 13. Research Analysis & Inferences

### 13.1 Immersion into the research
- **Insider access as a strength:** I work inside BLUORNG's content team, so I can shadow real planning meetings, shoots and ad launches. My inside role is also a **bias risk**. I control for it with external practitioner interviews, the audience survey, and by separating *observation* from *interpretation* in my shadowing log.
- **Data access:** with mentor approval, export IG Insights and Meta Ads data (indexed) for the content audit.

### 13.2 Defining (redefining) the problem
**Original brief:** "A platform to help BLUORNG know what content to make for organic & Meta ads."

**Redefined brief (after research, update with your evidence):**
> Design **BLUPRINT**, a decision and ideation tool for small, drop-led fashion content teams. It **(1)** learns which *types* of content work for the brand from its own organic and paid data, **(2)** turns that into on-brand recommendations for Instagram series/IPs and Meta ad angles, and **(3)** converts chosen ideas into shootable briefs planned around the drop calendar, so one shoot can feed both organic and paid.

*What changed:* the focus moved from "suggest content" to **"connect insight → idea → brief → shoot"**, and **brand guardrails** and **explainability** were added as must-haves.

### 13.3 Elaborated scope of design
**End goal:** a tested hi-fi prototype of BLUPRINT (web dashboard + mobile companion) and a design system.

**Modules in scope:**

1. **Pulse (Insights):** what worked and why, by content type.
2. **Ideas (Recommendations):** organic IPs & formats, paid angles & hooks.
3. **Briefs:** auto-generated shot lists, references, ratios, captions.
4. **Plan (Drop calendar):** organic + paid on one timeline.
5. **Brand DNA:** guardrails, tone, do's & don'ts.

**User segments who benefit and can afford it:** BLUORNG's in-house team (sponsor), then other Indian premium D2C fashion brands with 5–50 person teams that spend on Meta ads. They already pay for tools like Later or Motion, so a focused SaaS tier is realistic.

**How we proceed:** research findings → personas → problem statement → design goals → ideation → 3 concepts → final concept → IA → wireframes → UI → prototype → testing.

### 13.4 Environment & context
| Dimension | Context |
|---|---|
| **Where** | BLUORNG office/studio (planning on laptop), shoot locations and stores (mobile, on the go), founder reviewing on phone |
| **When** | Weekly content planning, monthly reviews, **drop weeks** (high pressure, high volume), ad refresh cycles (every 2–4 weeks) |
| **Devices** | Desktop/laptop for planning & analysis; **mobile** for briefs on set, approvals, quick idea capture |
| **Data environment** | Instagram Graph API, Meta Marketing API, Shopify; trend inputs (audio, culture) |
| **Constraints** | Meta API limits & policies; data privacy; small team (time-poor); premium brand (no discount-led content); production budgets; Indian network conditions on set |
| **Beneficiaries (max benefit)** | Content team (time, clarity), performance marketer (better CPA via diversity), founders (brand control, ROI), makers (clear briefs), audience (better content) |

### 13.5 Factors & constraints → fine-tuned brief
| Factor | Constraint | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Technology | AI needs enough tagged data; cold start | Manual tagging + templates at start; learning improves over time |
| Business | Must show ROI quickly | "Quick wins" view (promote organic winners) |
| Brand | Premium, "cool", never desperate | Brand-fit score; no discount-style suggestions by default |
| People | Creative team resists "algorithm telling us what to do" | AI as co-pilot: suggest, explain, let humans decide |
| Time | Drop weeks are chaotic | Mobile, fast, glanceable |
| Ethics | Creator/model rights, data privacy | Usage-rights field in briefs; no audience PII |

**Sectors needing immediate attention before ideation:** (1) a shared **content taxonomy**, (2) the **insight → idea** link, (3) the **organic ↔ paid** bridge.
