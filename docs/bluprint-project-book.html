1. Project Proposal

1.1 Working title

BLUPRINT: a content intelligence and ideation platform that helps BLUORNG decide what to make for Instagram (organic) and Meta Ads (paid).

Name logic: "BLU" (from BLUORNG) + "PRINT" (blueprint): a blueprint for every piece of content before a camera is switched on.

1.2 Sponsor / context

  • Industry partner: BLUORNG, a New Delhi streetwear label (founded 2020 by Siddhant Sabharwal and Mokam Singh). Named GQ India's Streetwear Label of the Year for 2023. It runs stores in Delhi NCR and Mumbai and sells online, and it has done brand collaborations such as Jägermeister and Budweiser.
  • My role inside the partner: I work inside BLUORNG's content team, which lets me observe directly how content gets planned, shot, posted and promoted. In this project I am the UI/UX designer: I research the content workflow and design a digital product that improves it.
  • Mentors: Industry mentor (BLUORNG brand/marketing lead) + University guide.

Confidentiality: Any internal data (Instagram Insights, Meta Ads Manager numbers, sales figures, internal docs) must only be used with the written OK of the industry mentor. Anonymise or index numbers (e.g. "Reel A = 100, Reel B = 240") where needed.

1.3 Background

BLUORNG grew from an Instagram-first niche label into a fast-rising fashion brand. Its content is a mix of:

  • Organic content: drop teasers, lookbooks, campaign films, behind-the-scenes, store-launch content, collaborations, community/culture posts.
  • Inorganic (paid) content: Meta ads (Instagram + Facebook) for drops, retargeting, store footfall and evergreen products.

Today, what to make next is decided mostly from experience, gut feel, trend-watching and scattered numbers. The numbers sit in Instagram Insights, Meta Ads Manager and Shopify, while the conversations happen on WhatsApp and in meetings. Nothing connects "what we posted / ran" to "what worked / sold" to "what we should make next".

1.4 Problem (initial brief)

Content teams at drop-led streetwear brands like BLUORNG don't have a structured, evidence-backed way to decide what content to produce, both for organic Instagram (formats, series/IPs, stories) and for Meta ads (creative angles, hooks, variations). As a result, creative effort and ad spend are spent on guesswork, wins are not repeated systematically, and organic and paid content run as separate worlds.

1.5 Aim

Design a platform that turns performance data + brand identity + trend signals into clear, brand-safe content recommendations and ready-to-shoot briefs, for both organic and paid content.

1.6 Objectives

Objective 1
Understand the workflow

How the content and performance teams ideate, plan, shoot, publish and review.

Objective 2
Find the gaps

The pain points and decision gaps inside that workflow.

Objective 3
Know the audience

What BLUORNG's audience actually engages with, and why.

Objective 4
Build a shared language

A taxonomy of formats, series, hooks and angles the platform can learn from.

Objective 5
Choose a direction

Generate and evaluate three concepts, then select one.

Objective 6
Design and test

IA, flows, wireframes, UI and a prototype, tested with real users.

1.7 Scope

In scope
  • Instagram organic ideas: Reels, carousels, stories and series like ASMR or story experiences
  • Meta ads ideas: angles, hooks, variations and fatigue alerts
  • Insights on what worked, briefs and a drop calendar
  • Desktop web app plus a mobile companion for set and approvals
Out of scope
  • Building the machine-learning model or backend
  • Auto-posting or buying ads inside the tool
  • YouTube and X, kept as future scope
  • Redesigning the e-commerce site

1.8 Expected outcome

Hi-fi prototype

BLUPRINT web app and mobile companion, researched and usability-tested.

Design system and IA

Documented structure, navigation, labels and UI components.

Usable recommendations

Validated changes BLUORNG can apply even without building the product.

6. Understanding the System (System Mapping)

6.1 Current-state content system ("AS-IS")

Observations:

  1. Broken feedback loop: measurement data rarely flows back into ideation in a structured way.
  2. Organic ↔ paid silo: the organic team and the performance marketer use different tools and metrics, and organic winners are rarely promoted systematically.
  3. Single point of decision: founder approval is the bottleneck, and the criteria are taste-based and undocumented.
  4. Memory loss: what worked last drop lives in people's heads and chat history.
  5. Production is expensive: every shoot day matters, so wrong ideas are costly.

11.1 Survey A: Practitioners (Google Form)

Intro: "I'm a design student researching how fashion brands decide what content to create. 10 minutes, anonymous."

Section 1: About you

  1. Your role? (Social media manager / Content strategist / Cinematographer-photographer / Editor / Performance marketer / Founder / Agency / Other)
  2. Brand type? (Streetwear / Premium fashion / Mass fashion / Other D2C)
  3. Team size? (1–3 / 4–10 / 11–25 / 25+)
  4. Years of experience? (<1 / 1–3 / 3–5 / 5+)

Section 2: How you decide content

  1. How do you mainly decide what content to make? (multi-select: Gut/experience, Trends/audio, Competitors, Past performance data, Founder/manager decides, Agency suggests, Audience comments/DMs)
  2. How often do you look at content performance data? (Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely / Never)
  3. When you look at data, do you use it to plan the next content? (Always / Often / Sometimes / Rarely / Never)
  4. Which tools do you use? (IG Insights, Meta Ads Manager, Meta Business Suite, Later/Hootsuite/Sprout, Notion, Google Sheets, WhatsApp, ChatGPT, Motion/Foreplay, Other)
  5. Rate (1–5): "I know clearly which type of content works best for our brand."
  6. Rate (1–5): "Coming up with fresh content ideas every week is hard."

Section 3: Organic ↔ paid

  1. How often are your best organic posts turned into ads? (Always … Never)
  2. Rate (1–5): "Organic and paid teams plan content together."
  3. (Perf. marketers) How many new ad creatives do you test per month? (0–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / 10+)
  4. (Perf. marketers) Rate (1–5): "I get enough creative variations to test."
  5. How do you notice an ad is fatigued? (open)

Section 4: Brief & production

  1. How are content briefs usually shared? (Verbal, WhatsApp, Doc/Notion, Deck, No brief)
  2. How often do vague briefs cause re-shoots or re-edits? (Very often … Never)
  3. Rate (1–5): "Drop weeks are stressful for content."

Section 5: Pain & wishes

  1. Rank your top 3 problems: (Idea block, Not knowing what works, Too little time, Too few ad variations, Unclear briefs, Approval delays, Staying on-brand, Reporting takes time)
  2. Would you trust content suggestions from an AI tool? (Yes / Only if it explains why / Only as inspiration / No)
  3. What must such a tool NEVER do? (open)
  4. If you had a magic content assistant, what's the first thing it would do for you? (open)

11.3 Interview guide (team / practitioners): 30–45 min

  1. Warm-up: Walk me through your role and a normal week.
  2. Last piece of content: Tell me about the last Reel/ad you worked on. Where did the idea come from? Who decided? What happened after?
  3. Data: What numbers do you check? Show me (screen-share). What do you do with them?
  4. Wins & losses: Tell me about a post/ad that did really well. Why do you think it did? Did you make more like it? And one that flopped?
  5. Organic ↔ paid: How does content move from Instagram to ads (or the reverse)?
  6. Briefs: Show me a recent brief. What's usually missing?
  7. Drops: Walk me through the last drop week, day by day.
  8. Brand: How do you know something is "BLUORNG" or "not BLUORNG"?
  9. AI/tools: Have you used ChatGPT or any tool for ideas? What was good or bad?
  10. Magic wand: If one part of this process could be fixed, which?

Founder variant: Add "What makes you approve or reject a content idea?" and "How do you judge if content is working: sales, buzz, or feel?"

11.4 Focus group guide (audience): 60 min

  1. Ice-breaker: Your favourite streetwear brand on IG and why.
  2. Card sort: Show 15 printed BLUORNG and peer-brand posts/ads → group them into "would watch / save / share / buy / skip".
  3. Discuss: What makes a brand post feel premium vs cheap? Ad vs content?
  4. Show 3 ad styles (studio lookbook vs creator UGC vs ASMR detail) and discuss reactions.
  5. Co-create: "Design BLUORNG's next IG series."

12.4 Qualitative analysis method

  1. Transcribe interviews → pull quotes on sticky notes (Miro/FigJam).
  2. Affinity map into themes (use the clusters A–H in Part 3 as a starting frame and add new ones).
  3. Count frequency (how many participants mention each theme) → priority.
  4. Turn each theme into an insight statement: "[User] needs [need] because [insight]."

12.5 Expected insight statements (validate or rewrite with your data)

  1. "Data without direction." The team sees numbers, but nobody turns them into "make more of X, less of Y".
  2. "The idea treadmill." A drop-led brand needs constant novelty, and idea block peaks right before drops.
  3. "Two teams, one feed." Organic and paid share the same audience but not the same plan.
  4. "Same shoot, same ads." Ad variations are limited by what was shot, so planning ad angles before the shoot is the real fix.
  5. "Brief is a WhatsApp voice note." Makers lose time to unclear briefs and re-edits.
  6. "Protect the cool." Any suggestion that feels mass, salesy or trend-chasing will be rejected.
  7. "Show me why." Trust in AI depends on explanation, examples from the brand's own past, and the ability to edit.

22. Final Concept: BLUPRINT = Studio core + Pulse brain + Drop Room spine

Selected direction: Concept B (Studio) as the core experience, powered by A's insight engine (so ideas come from real data, not generic AI) and organised on C's drop timeline (so it fits how BLUORNG works).

22.1 Justification

  1. Against research needs: It addresses the top 3 priority areas (knowing what works → Pulse; ideation → Studio; organic ↔ paid → Drop Room) and the adoption conditions (explainable, brand-safe, editable).
  2. Against viability: - Technology: Instagram Graph API and Meta Marketing API provide post and ad metrics. LLMs can produce ideas and briefs. Tagging can start manually or semi-automatically. - Cost/time: An MVP can be built as a web app on existing APIs. No custom hardware. - Process: It fits the existing weekly planning and drop cycle; it doesn't replace people. - Modularity: Modules (Pulse, Ideas, Briefs, Plan, Brand DNA) can ship one at a time. - Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA, mobile companion for on-set use.
